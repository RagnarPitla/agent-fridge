# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be the kind of person other people want to share a fridge with.

## The longer version

This project exists because coordination is hard and people step on each other by
accident. The same is true of conversation. Assume the other person is trying to
help, say what you mean, and fix it when you get it wrong.

**Expected:**

- Assume good faith. Most conflicts are a misread, not an attack.
- Be specific. "This will lose data when two processes race" is useful. "This is
  bad" is not.
- Critique the design, the code, or the argument. Not the person.
- Accept that "no" is a legitimate answer, especially about scope. A maintainer
  declining a feature is not a personal judgement of you.
- Give people room to be new. Everyone was.
- When you are wrong, say so plainly and move on. It costs nothing.

**Not accepted:**

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption.
- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discrimination or derogatory comments about who someone is: their race,
  ethnicity, nationality, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation,
  disability, age, body size, appearance, religion, or class.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing threads, brigading, or bad-faith argument.
- Encouraging or excusing any of the above.

## Where this applies

Every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, the
wiki, and any chat or event where you are representing the project. It also
applies when your behaviour elsewhere makes a project space unsafe for someone.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer at
<https://github.com/RagnarPitla>. If the problem involves the maintainer,
say so in your report and it will be handled by an uninvolved contributor.

Reports are read by as few people as possible. The reporter's identity is not
shared with the person reported without the reporter's consent. Retaliation
against a reporter is itself a serious violation.

Include what happened, where, when, and links if they exist. You do not have to
have a legal-grade case; if something felt wrong, that is enough to report it.

## What happens next

Maintainers will look at the report and respond proportionately. In rough order:

1. **A private word.** An explanation of what was wrong and what to do instead.
   Most things end here.
2. **A public correction.** A note in the thread, and usually an apology requested.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces, with the terms stated in writing.
4. **A permanent ban** for sustained or severe behaviour: harassment, threats, or
   repeating something after a warning.

Maintainers may edit, hide, or delete comments, commits, code, issues, and other
contributions that violate this document, and will say that they have done so.
